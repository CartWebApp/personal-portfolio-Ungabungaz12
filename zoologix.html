Project Case Study: Zoologix

The Goal:

Zoologix was an enrichment tracking application for the Fresno Chaffee Zoo, where, at my time at the Center for Advanced Research and Technology (CART), I worked on the user-interface of the “Add/Edit Enrichment” Section with a group of 4 other students.

The Technology Stack:

  • Tools: Figma for extensive user interface development options.

Concepts Learned

  • Auto Layout: I strengthened my knowledge of Figma’s design tools to utilize “Auto-Layout”, ensuring consistent design throughout all pages.
  • Extensions: I sped up my development process by using community made extensions to solve problems that would have taken a long time otherwise.
  • Accessibility Considerations: I learned the importance of accessibility throughout my design process, using WCAG acceptable color palettes and ease of use improvements.

Challenges & Solutions

  • The Problem: Within the Figma design, the screens that we were using per action taken were cluttered, and hard to immediately edit due to the sheer amount of them.
  • The Solution: I worked with my team members to develop a solution that only uses one screen, and uses components instead, improving readability and editability throughout the design process.
  • The Problem: We did not know what exactly the zoo needed, what they did not need, and what they wanted out of our section.
  • The Solution: CART sent us out to the real location of the Fresno Chaffee Zoo to conduct an interview with the zoo specialists to determine what they wanted/needed out of the app, and what their current app does well that we can implement in our own designs, synthesizing this data to tailor the app towards the people that are going to use it.
  • The Problem: We didn’t know if the designs we had were acceptable, after developing them.
  • The Solution: CART brought in real user experience designers to look over our designs and critique them.

Habits of Mind in Action:

  • Persistence: We spent a lot of time on the many different screens, however, after noticing how cluttered it was to have all these screens separate, we had to scrap the initial design to create a much more readable and editable design.
  • Communication: Throughout the process, we communicated both with the zoo, and with each other on different solutions to the same problems, synthesizing everything to create the best possible design that satisfied all parties
